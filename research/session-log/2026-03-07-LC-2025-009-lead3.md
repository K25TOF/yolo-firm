### 1. Divergent Trade Isolation

**The trade count discrepancy (+93) is the first thing to resolve.**

Config B has 93 more trades than Config A. The Engineer noted this occurs because ATR exits earlier on some positions, freeing capacity for re-entry signals. This means the two configs are **not directly comparable at the trade level** — Config B has a structurally different trade sequence on those 93 ticker-days.

**Back-calculating what we can:**

The +1.63pp WR improvement across 6,172→6,265 trades gives us:
- Config A winners: 6,172 × 0.2794 ≈ **1,724 wins**
- Config B winners: 6,265 × 0.2957 ≈ **1,852 wins**
- Net new winners: **+128**

Config B won 128 more trades on 93 more total trades. This means:
- The 93 additional trades (re-entries unlocked by early ATR exit) have a WR of approximately **128/93 ≈ 138%** — which is impossible.

This tells me the +128 winner gain isn't coming only from the 93 new trades. **ATR is also converting losses to wins within the shared trade population** — exits that would have ended as losses under EMA-only are being cut earlier by ATR and becoming smaller losses... wait, those would still be losses. Let me re-examine.

**Revised interpretation:** Some Config A losses (held longer, ended below entry) become Config B wins (ATR exit fires before the loss materialises, or the ATR exit allows a re-entry that wins). The +128 winners come from a combination of: (a) trades that exit earlier via ATR at a profit, and (b) new trades unlocked by early exits.

**I observe:** Without direct CSV access to isolate the exact divergent pairs, I cannot give a precise divergent trade count. The +93 trade count difference is a lower bound on divergent pairs — the true divergent count (same pair, different exit) is likely higher but partially masked by the re-entry effect.

**Confidence: LOW on exact divergent count.** The +93 trade count asymmetry makes standard divergence isolation ambiguous without CSV queries.
