## HYP-024: VWAP Distance as Entry Quality Filter

**ID:** HYP-024

**Question:** 
Does filtering entries to those occurring near or above VWAP (rather than overextended) improve win rate and reduce loss severity on momentum trades?

**Signal Definition:**
- **Entry gate:** Only take vol_filter entry signals where price at entry bar is within X% of VWAP
  - Test variants: +0% (at/below VWAP), +2%, +5%, +10% distance
- **Rationale:** Entries near VWAP = stock in trend. Entries >10% above VWAP = stock extended, vulnerable to reversion. This filters spike-chase patterns without using time or other indicators.

**Expected Outcome:**
- Win rate improves from baseline skip-first (34%) to 40%+
- Average winner size unchanged (~+24.9% per big winner)
- Average loser magnitude shrinks (fewer -7% to -10% losses)
- Trade count reduces by 20-30% (filters overextended entries)
- Total P&L stays positive (fewer trades but better quality)

**Falsification Criteria:**
1. Win rate worsens (< 34%)
2. Total P&L drops below +40% (on 17-day dataset equivalent)
3. Profit factor worsens (fewer wins don't offset fewer losers)
4. VWAP filter removes more winners than losers (evidence of false positive filtering)

---

## Why This Matters

**From prior research:**
- EXP-012 (trade-level pattern analysis): VWAP distance showed marginal separation (winners 14.7% above VWAP vs losers 12.4%)
- EXP-017 (losing trade anatomy): VWAP < 20% filter improved PnL by +3.2pp on 82 trades
- EXP-021 (6-day multi-day): No explicit VWAP testing, but pattern emerged that first entries on extended moves lose more

**The insight:** We're currently entering momentum trades at all VWAP distances indiscriminately. The data hints that filtering out the most overextended entries might reduce the "spike-chase" failure mode (entering at peaks that immediately revert), which is our #1 loser pattern.

**Why not just rely on skip-first?** Skip-first is powerful (filters first signals which are often spikes), but it's coarse — it rejects all first signals even on legitimate trending setups. VWAP distance is a *valuation filter* — it says "this stock is in a healthy trend, not a climactic spike." These could be complementary.

---

## What I'm NOT Claiming

- VWAP distance will beat skip-first outright (it probably won't)
- VWAP is a universal momentum signal (it's not — it's a trend-context filter)
- We should abandon skip-first for VWAP (they could stack together)
- This will solve the high-variance problem (big winners will still drive edge; losers will just be slightly smaller)

---

## Why I'm Uncertain

1. **EXP-017 showed only +3.2pp improvement** on an 82-trade sample with one filter variant. Very marginal. Might not reproduce at scale.
2. **VWAP itself is computed from aggregated trades.** On low-liquidity sub-$1 stocks (our sweet spot), VWAP can be noisy or manipulated.
3. **The skip-first edge is already extremely strong** (+75.2pp improvement EXP-022 → EXP-021 baseline). Adding a second filter might hit diminishing returns — removing winners along with losers.
4. **Signal coupling risk:** If we filter for VWAP distance AND skip first entries AND require non-accelerating EMA gap, we might end up with <10 trades across 17 days — not enough to validate statistical significance.

---

## Research Direction

I recommend **Engineer run a clean test** on the 17-day batch dataset (EXP-023 baseline: 93 trades, 34% WR, +67.4%):
- Baseline: skip-first vol_filter (current)
- Variant A: skip-first + VWAP >= -0% (at/below VWAP)
- Variant B: skip-first + VWAP >= +2%
- Variant C: skip-first + VWAP >= +5%
- Variant D: skip-first + VWAP >= +10%

**Judge on divergent trades only** — where skip-first trades but VWAP filter blocks. What's the P&L of blocked trades? Do they tend to be losers (evidence of filtering noise) or a mix (evidence of false positives)?

## 3-Amigos Concerns — Let Me Raise My Hand First

### Concern #1: VWAP Computation on Sub-$1 Stocks

**The risk:** Our sweet-spot tickers (MOBX, DXST, CCHH) trade $0.10–$1.50. At this price range:
- Bid-ask spread is often 1–3 cents (1–5% of price)
- VWAP aggregates all intraday trades, but on low-volume pre-momentum bars, it's based on minimal trades
- A single large block trade can shift VWAP by 0.5–2% instantly
- Result: VWAP at bar N might be meaningfully different from bar N+1 due to single large order, not trend change

**Impact on test:** Filtering by VWAP distance might be filtering on noise rather than trend context. The backtest will use close-of-bar VWAP (from completed 1-min candle), but at entry decision time (first few seconds of the next bar), VWAP hasn't updated yet — there's a timing mismatch.

**Do we proceed anyway?** Yes — this is a valid limitation but doesn't invalidate the test. We'll capture it in caveats.

---

### Concern #2: VWAP vs EMA Gap — Potential Redundancy

**The tension:** EMA gap already encodes trend direction and momentum strength. VWAP encodes trend context (price relative to average).

- EMA gap > 3.0 + high volume = momentum + conviction
- VWAP > price by 5% = stock has been hammered, now recovering (or is very early in a spike)
- These are somewhat orthogonal, but both are "trend context" signals

**Risk:** We might be filtering away trades that pass EMA gap (is real momentum) but fail VWAP (is extended). The Analyst expects this to reduce losers, but we could be removing winners too.

**Mitigation:** Judge on **divergent trades only**, as Analyst requested. We'll see exactly which trades VWAP blocks and their P&L.

---

### Concern #3: Analyst's Confidence is MEDIUM — This is Exploratory

The hypothesis is sound in theory, but EXP-017 showed only +3.2pp improvement and came with caveats ("sample sizes <20 per bucket"). 

**Red flag:** If we see <+5pp improvement across 93 trades, is the edge real or noise? I'll flag this in results, but wanted to surface it now so we agree on the bar.
