**[SCOUT FIND]** — **Maróy (SSRN:5095349, Jan 2025)** — Intraday Momentum Exit Strategies

**[SCOUT FIND]** — Practitioner consensus on RSI divergence as exhaustion signal:
- Indicators like RSI divergence measure the speed and magnitude of price changes. When price pushes to a new extreme but RSI no longer confirms it — RSI divergence — it can indicate weakening momentum. These clues are not guarantees, but they often signal that a trend is tiring.

**[SCOUT FIND]** — Critical caution on RSI overbought/oversold signals:

## Topic 3: Momentum Exhaustion Patterns

### Key Sources

**[SCOUT FIND]** — **Chabot, Ghysels & Jagannathan (NBER WP 20660, 2014)**
- Source type: **NBER Working Paper** (high quality; cited in Journal of Finance)
- Finding: The momentum strategy exposed investors to large losses (crashes). Momentum crashes were predictable — more likely when momentum recently performed well, interest rates were relatively low, or momentum had recently outperformed the stock market.
- Relevance: Momentum exhaustion/crash timing is predictable from prior momentum strength — directly relevant to identifying when to apply exit guards.
- **Confidence: HIGH** (NBER; long data series 1867–2012)

**[SCOUT FIND]** — **J.P. Morgan Quantitative & Derivatives Strategy (2015)** — Momentum Across Asset Classes
- Source type: **Institutional practitioner paper** (high credibility; not peer-reviewed)
- Finding: The return profile of momentum strategies is characterized by long periods of outperformance, with increased risk during market turning points that occur when a trend is reversed. For most trend-following strategies, tail risk and negative skewness are more likely to occur during market turning points and periods of increased volatility.
- Relevance: Confirms momentum exhaustion is a real regime phenomenon, not random noise.
- **Confidence: HIGH** (institutional; extensive data)

**[SCOUT FIND]** — **Volume exhaustion signal (Wikipedia Volume Analysis / practitioner consensus)**
- Finding: Exceedingly extreme changes in volume (five to ten times normal) are referred to as exhaustion — there will often be no one left to participate, and the price will stop moving in its previous trajectory. A sharp increase in price with a sharp increase in volume might signal that all bulls have been exhausted, making the resultant price trend bearish.
- Relevance: Volume spike exhaustion is an established concept — volume decay after a run signals diminishing momentum.
- **Confidence: MEDIUM** (widely cited principle; limited rigorous academic test in our specific context)

**[SCOUT FIND]** — Momentum divergence patterns:
- In a healthy trend, price changes are supported by consistent momentum. As the trend ages, momentum often wanes, signaling fewer new participants and weaker follow-through.
- A classic sign of exhaustion is when price makes new highs or lows, but momentum indicators fail to confirm these moves, creating divergence. Volume anomalies — shrinking volume in the direction of the trend, or sudden spikes counter to it — can suggest diminishing participation and rising opposition.
- **Confidence: MEDIUM** (practitioner consensus; no specific peer-reviewed study on intraday small-cap application)

**[SCOUT FIND]** — **Springer Financial Innovation (peer-reviewed, Jan 2025)**
- Finding: Although momentum strategies result in abnormal profitability, concerns persist regarding their reliability due to significant volatility and susceptibility to substantial losses. The volatile profits are due to statistical analyses that assume the persistence of past patterns, leading to unreliable results in out-of-sample scenarios when underlying mechanisms evolve.
- Relevance: Systematic warning that momentum exhaustion patterns may not persist out-of-sample.
- **Confidence: HIGH** (peer-reviewed journal)

### Key Sources

**[SCOUT FIND]** — **Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz (SSRN:4729284, 2024)** — direct RVOL threshold evidence
- Finding: Best performing stocks for the n-minute ORB strategies used a Relative Volume of at least 100% (i.e., RVOL ≥ 2.0) as the filter threshold. This is the explicit Stocks in Play filter that drives their alpha.

- The threshold for "significantly" above average volume differs for each investor. Many day traders look for an RVOL over 2.0 before investing.
- It's important to be wary of extreme RVOL values. If the RVOL suddenly spikes to 4.0 or more, especially while the security is overbought/oversold, that could foreshadow a trend reversal.
- **Confidence: MEDIUM** (practitioner consensus; no rigorous academic test of specific threshold)

**[SCOUT FIND]** — Small-cap RVOL context — critical nuance:
- The above thresholds are for large, liquid stocks. If you're talking about small cap stocks where news hits the wire and a stock doubles in price, an RVOL of 2 would be nothing. Those kinds of stocks can easily hit 10+ RVOL.
- Relevance: **Critical for our team's context.** RVOL 2.0 is a minimum floor for large-caps; small-cap momentum candidates should use a higher threshold or compare within small-cap universe.
- **Confidence: MEDIUM** (practitioner; community consensus)

**[SCOUT FIND]** — Time-of-day adjusted RVOL as best practice:
- While classic RVOL aggregates volume across the entire trading day, RVOL-TOD compares volume to the average at a specific time of day. This is useful for short-term traders, as volume can be much higher at the beginning or end of the trading day than at other times of day.
- Relevance: RVOL-TOD is the technically correct implementation for intraday 1-min bar scanning. Raw RVOL will overstate early-morning readings.
- **Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH** (established best practice; practitioner reference)

### EXP-016: CONDITIONAL → SALVAGE (direction only)?

**Optimist's argument:** Direction confirmed at scale by LC-2025-009 (+1.63pp WR on ~6,275 trades). The p=0.084 result concerns magnitude, not direction.

**Challenger assessment:**

I need to be precise here. The Statistician reported p=0.084 on the 19-divergent-trade comparison for the specific ATR variant (p14, N=2.0). The Optimist is proposing to use LC-2025-009's +1.63pp result as the directional confirmation.

**Problem 1:** LC-2025-009 tested ATR exit on the **broad momentum universe** (~6,275 trades). EXP-016 tested it on the **6-date, 82-trade, 49-ticker hand-picked set**. The large-n result uses a different universe. We cannot directly transfer the +1.63pp directional confirmation to the EXP-016 configuration.

**Problem 2:** +1.63pp WR improvement on ~6,275 trades. The Statistician noted this is "real but insufficient alone." That is a directional blip, not a confirmed edge. At +1.63pp on a ~32% base WR, the absolute effect is clinically marginal. Confirming "direction" on an effect this small is not analytically useful — it doesn't tell you whether to use ATR exits in production.

**Problem 3:** The 20-variant sweep in EXP-016 (Statistician flagged: ~1 expected false positive at p<0.05). The "winning" variant p14, N=2.0 was post-hoc selected. This selection was never corrected.

**My position:** The Optimist's reframe is logically appealing but merges two different datasets to construct a "confirmed direction" that neither dataset alone supports. The correct classification is:

- EXP-016: **INCONCLUSIVE** (upgrading from CONDITIONAL — the Statistician's p=0.084 and 20-variant sweep formally disqualify the specific result)
- LC-2025-009 ATR result: **SALVAGE — directional only, effect too small to act on alone**. The +1.63pp at n=6,275 is the most reliable ATR finding in the series. It says ATR exit is not harmful and marginally positive. That is the only defensible claim.

**Verdict: EXP-016 → INCONCLUSIVE. LC-2025-009 ATR → SALVAGE (directional, small effect, cannot support production decision alone).**

### EXP-021/022/023 Skip-First: SALVAGE → what caveats?

**Evidence summary from Statistician:**
- Three-step chain (discovery → prospective → replication): strongest methodological structure in the series
- All three steps: same Feb–Mar 2026 time window
- EXP-023: 11/16 losing days — heavily outlier-day dependent
- OOS requirement: ≥60–93 trades in a non-overlapping date range

**Challenger assessment:**

The three-step chain is real. The directional finding (skip-first entries substantially outperform first entries) is the most credible result in the EXP series. I am not blocking this.

However, the caveats must be stated precisely:

**Caveat 1 — Single time window:** All three experiments are in the same Feb–Mar 2026 window. A "three-step chain" on overlapping data is not three independent replications — it is one finding described three times with increasing n. The correct characterisation is: *one well-constructed in-sample result on a single time window.*

**Caveat 2 — Outlier-day dependency:** 11/16 losing days means the strategy loses money on 69% of days. Total positive P&L is driven by 5 days. This is not a consistent edge — it is an asymmetric return profile. That may be acceptable depending on position sizing, but it must be stated explicitly. An investor deploying this strategy on a random 20-day window would lose money on roughly 11 of those days.

**Caveat 3 — Universe overlap with ORB research:** The multibagger universe (LC-2025-014) and the skip-first universe (EXP-023) have not been confirmed as the same or different populations. If skip-first was validated on a broader scanner universe and ORB on the multibagger-filtered universe, the interaction of skip-first + ORB filters on the multibagger universe is untested.

**Verdict: SALVAGE — with three explicit caveats stated above. OOS validation is a hard requirement before any production discussion. The "highest-confidence finding in the series" characterisation is accepted, but must not be read as "ready for deployment."**
