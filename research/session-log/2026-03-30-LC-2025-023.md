### Question 3: Null Hypothesis and Sample Size

**Optimist's null hypothesis is correctly stated:** "Pre-market signals do not improve 100%+ intraday range hit rate above base rate."

**But the base rate calculation is wrong.**

Optimist states: 1,922 days / 110,240 scanned = 1.7%.

The 1,922 / 110,240 figure represents the fraction of ticker-days in the multibagger universe (stocks selected *because* they had 100%+ days), not the fraction of the broader market.

The true base rate for pre-market scanning must be computed from the **unfiltered scanner output** — all stocks passing basic price/volume thresholds on a given morning, regardless of outcome. **Demand from Manager/Statistician:** What is the base rate for 100%+ intraday range among *all* stocks passing basic gap criteria (gap ≥ 5%, PM vol ≥ 50K) — not just the 450-stock confirmed multibagger universe? This is the correct denominator for scanner performance measurement.

### Completing Blocking Issue B: "100%+ Intraday Range" vs "100%+ From Open" — Two Different Research Problems

The session question asks for identification of "100%+ intraday mover candidates." This phrase is ambiguous and the answer determines the entire research design. There are three distinct definitions:

**Definition 1: Intraday range ≥ 100%**
`(Day_High - Day_Low) / Day_Low ≥ 1.0`

This is the definition used in LC-2025-014 (the 450-stock multibagger universe). It includes stocks that opened high, sold off hard, then recovered — the full range from low to high regardless of direction. A stock that opens at $2.00, drops to $1.00, and recovers to $2.10 qualifies. This is a volatility measure, not a directional momentum measure.

**Definition 2: Open-to-high ≥ 100%**
`(Day_High - Open) / Open ≥ 1.0`

This is the directional momentum question — "did the stock double from where it opened?" A pre-market scanner identifying morning runners is implicitly targeting this definition. You are long from near the open and need the stock to continue moving up. This is the tradeable question.

**Definition 3: Close-to-open gap + continuation ≥ 100%**
`(Day_High - Prior_Close) / Prior_Close ≥ 1.0`

This includes the overnight gap component plus intraday continuation. Relevant if the strategy enters pre-market or at open on the gap.

**Why this matters — it is not a minor definitional quibble:**

LC-2025-014 used Definition 1. A stock that gaps up 60% pre-market and then falls back 50% from the open still qualifies under Definition 1 (the range is massive), but a scanner that bought the open would have lost money. If the scanner research trains on the LC-2025-014 universe (Definition 1), but the trading strategy needs Definition 2, the training data and the deployment target are misaligned.

**Specific risk:** Low-float stocks frequently exhibit massive intraday ranges (Definition 1) driven by both the initial spike AND the subsequent crash. A pre-market scanner optimised to find Definition 1 events will find exactly the stocks that spike and crash — which are precisely the stocks that kill a long momentum strategy if you enter too late.

**Demand from Manager:** Confirm which definition of "100%+" is the research target. If it is Definition 2 (open-to-high), then the training dataset must be rebuilt from the 450-stock universe to exclude stocks where the high was set in the first 15 minutes and the remainder of the day was a decline. This is a non-trivial data recut.

### Blocking Issue C: The Scanner Output ≠ Trading Signal

This was not raised by Optimist and I consider it a design-level gap in the entire research plan.

A pre-market scanner answers: **"Which stocks are likely to move 100%+ today?"**

A trading signal answers: **"When do I enter, at what price, with what size, and when do I exit?"**

The research plan as designed conflates these two questions. Optimist's hypotheses define scanner criteria but treat a "pass" from the scanner as equivalent to a trade. They are not equivalent. Consider:

- A scanner fires on 20 stocks per morning. Which one do you trade? All 20? Rank order?
- The scanner fires at 9:15 ET. Entry is at 9:30 open. The stock may have already moved 40% in pre-market — is the entry still valid?
- A stock passes HYP-PM-1 (Tier 1 catalyst, gap ≥10%, PM vol ≥50K) but then gaps down 20% between 9:15 and 9:29. Does the scanner signal persist?

The research plan must explicitly state: **the scanner is a candidate identification tool, not an entry trigger.** The output of this research is a filtered universe (daily watchlist), not a backtest-ready signal. Success metrics must be defined at the universe level (precision and recall), not the trade level (WR and PnL). These are separate research problems that must not be mixed.

**Consequence:** The "success metrics" proposed by Optimist — e.g., "≥ 15pp improvement in 100%+ hit rate" — are universe-level precision metrics. They are the correct metrics for this research phase. But Optimist also references WR and PnL comparisons from EXP-018 and EXP-023, which are trade-level metrics from a different strategy. These must not be conflated in the research brief. The scanner plan produces a watchlist. A separate entry/exit research phase converts that watchlist into trades.
