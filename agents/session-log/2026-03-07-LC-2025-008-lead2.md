## Audit — LC-2025-007 Gap Acceleration Filter on Vol_filter

### 1. Divergent Trade Analysis

**Total divergent set:** 6,347 − 88 = **6,259 trades** that Config A takes and Config B skips.

I observe the following from Engineer-reported aggregates:

| | Config A (all trades) | Config B (88 trades) | Implied divergent set (6,259 trades) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trade count | 6,347 | 88 | 6,259 |
| Win rate | 27.9% | 23.9% | ~28.0% (back-calculated) |
| Total PnL | -5,582.7% | -170.3% | ~-5,412.4% |

**Back-calculation on divergent WR:**

Config A winners: 6,347 × 27.9% ≈ 1,771 wins
Config B winners: 88 × 23.9% ≈ 21 wins
Divergent winners: 1,771 − 21 = ~1,750 wins out of 6,259 trades → **divergent WR ≈ 28.0%**

I observe: the 6,259 trades that Config B filtered out had a WR of approximately 28.0% — **slightly higher** than Config B's own 88 trades (23.9%). This is a critical finding.

**Conclusion from isolation testing:** Config B is not filtering out losers preferentially. It is filtering out trades that perform approximately as well as — or marginally better than — the trades it retains. The filter is removing signal, not noise.

---

### 2. WR Direction Check — Genuine Degradation or Sample Artefact?

I observe: Config B WR is 23.9% on 88 trades.

**Is 88 trades sufficient for a verdict?**

At 88 trades and ~24% WR, the binomial standard error ≈ √(0.24 × 0.76 / 88) ≈ **4.6pp**. The observed −4.0pp degradation vs Config A falls within one standard error. This means the WR degradation alone is **not statistically distinguishable from noise** at this sample size.

However, the back-calculated divergent WR (28.0%) vs Config B retained WR (23.9%) shows Config B selected an *inferior* subset — this is directionally consistent across both ways of looking at the data.

**Conclusion:** The −4.0pp WR is not statistically robust on its own. But the divergent trade analysis corroborates it. Combined: **genuine degradation signal, low-to-medium confidence**.

---

### 3. Threshold Hypothesis — `< 1.0` on `ema_gap_acceleration`

I agree with the Engineer's interpretation, and I can expand on the mechanism:

At the vol_filter entry point, `ema_gap(3,9)` has just crossed **above 3.0%**. By definition, the EMA gap is accelerating at the moment of entry — it just moved from <3.0% to >3.0%. For `ema_gap_acceleration` (3-bar ROC of ema_gap) to be **< 1.0%** at that same bar, the gap would need to have risen very gradually over the prior 3 bars — a near-flat approach to the 3.0% threshold.

This creates a **logical tension in the signal definition**: vol_filter requires a decisive crossing of 3.0%, but the acceleration filter demands that crossing arrived slowly. These two conditions are structurally rare to satisfy simultaneously at the 3.0% threshold level.

**Supporting evidence:** DIAG-A2 (LC-2025-002) tested this on 10 tickers × 16 dates (a hand-picked set with known runners) and got 109/239 trades passing. On 5,786 pairs (broad momentum universe), only 88/6,347 pass — a ratio approximately 20× more restrictive than expected by proportional scaling. This asymmetry suggests the filter's hit rate depends heavily on the character of the underlying universe, not just sample size.

**Implication:** The `< 1.0%` threshold was calibrated (implicitly) on a universe where gradual EMA gap buildups exist as a common pattern (the grinder-profile tickers in DIAG-A2). On the broad momentum universe — dominated by spike-profile entries — gradual approaches to 3.0% are uncommon. The threshold is not wrong in principle; it is wrong for this universe at this entry level.

The acceleration filter likely works on grinder-profile entries (low EMA gap, gradual approach). It does not transfer to the vol_filter entry regime (EMA gap crossing 3.0% decisively). These are two different filter contexts. The prior finding should not be cited as evidence for this application.
