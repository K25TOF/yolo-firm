**The single most important difference:** Impulse trades enter at the *peak of volume acceleration* (RVOL spike already firing), so they need fast exits to escape reversals; grinder trades enter *before volume peaks* (steady climb still building), so they can hold longer as long as price stays above VWAP.

**Why this matters for strategy selection:** Impulse = tight exits (EMA 1.5% to cut fake spikes), grinder = loose exits (EMA 2.0%+ to ride sustained trends). Same momentum signal, opposite exit discipline.

**One sentence version (if needed):** Impulse exploits the *spike moment*; grinder exploits the *continuation after the spike*.

I have three concerns that need flagging before I execute the grinder backtest:

1. **Sample size risk (tight 3-part AND filter).** Entry requires all three conditions: `ema_gap 1.0% AND price >= VWAP×1.02 AND gap_accel < 1.0%`. This threshold was picked from the outcome distribution, not from theory or cross-validated on held-out data. When I register and test `gap_accel < 1.0%`, I'll likely find it fits perfectly in-sample (Feb 24–Mar 3) but may fail on the Feb 10–23 out-of-sample subset.

3. **VWAP reliability on sub-$1 stocks.** HYP-024 highlighted noise: bid-ask spreads on MOBX, DXST, RYDE are 1-5% of price; single block trades move VWAP 0.5–2%. I can't confidently distinguish "real uptrend" from "a block trade moved VWAP."
