## Divergent Trade Analysis: RVOL 2.0 vs 5.0

Largest delta: threshold 5.0 removes 5,366 trades (-82%) but improves WR +3.51pp (29.57% → 33.08%) and cuts total loss by 75% (-4,531% → -577%).

**Back-calculated divergent set (trades only at 2.0, not at 5.0):**
```
divergent_trades = 6,565 - 1,199 = 5,366
divergent_winners = (6,565 × 0.2957) - (1,199 × 0.3308) 
                  = 1,942 - 397 = 1,545
divergent_WR = 1,545 / 5,366 = 28.80%
divergent_PnL = -4,531.0% - (-576.8%) = -3,954.2%
divergent_avg_PnL_per_trade = -3,954.2% / 5,366 = -0.737% per trade
```

**Interpretation:**
- The 5,366 "extra" trades at threshold 2.0 have 28.80% WR — *below* the 2.0-threshold baseline of 29.57%
- These trades are concentrated losers: -3,954.2% total, -0.737% per trade
- Filtering them out improves WR by +3.51pp (removing below-baseline-quality signals)
- But filtering them also removes 1,545 winners — they're not pure noise

**Verdict:** Higher threshold is selectively filtering low-conviction volume spikes that lose slightly more often than the baseline. The mechanism works as intended — it's just that *all signals* are losing money. Raising the volume bar improves quality marginally but doesn't fix the underlying problem.
